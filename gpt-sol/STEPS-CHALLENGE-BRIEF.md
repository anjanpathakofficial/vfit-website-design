# Vantage Fit Steps Challenge — solutions page brief

## The decision this page must win

An enterprise HR or wellbeing leader is not deciding whether walking is good. They are deciding whether a step challenge will attract more than the already active employees, remain credible across teams and time zones, and leave them with a result they can defend to leadership.

The page therefore sells a participation system, not a pedometer. Its job is to answer four questions in order:

1. Will employees actually join?
2. Can we choose a format that fits the behavior we want?
3. Can a small HR team run it across a complex workforce?
4. Will the results be fair, private, and reportable?

Primary conversion: **Book a demo**. Secondary conversion: **See pricing**.

## Product-truth audit

### What the legacy page gets right

- The category is immediately recognizable: steps, leaderboards, teams, and rewards.
- The current SEO focus, “corporate step challenge,” is worth retaining.
- A live product experience and named customer proof are more persuasive than abstract wellness claims.

### What it under-sells or mis-weights

- It treats a step challenge as one generic format. Current product documentation distinguishes Race, Streak, Journey, and E-Marathon, each with a different participation mechanic.
- It focuses on competitive energy without giving HR a way to choose between a short sprint, a daily habit, a visual journey, or a shared distance event.
- It says too little about operational control: audience selection, participant management, time-zone localization, notifications, live leaderboards, and exports.
- It leaves inclusion and trust implicit. A phone is enough; wearables are optional. Employees can hide from individual rankings while remaining in the challenge. HR can see participation and rankings, not individual health records.
- It presents features as isolated blocks rather than one operating loop: launch → join → sustain → prove.
- The proof is detached from the product story. This page uses one named, relevant customer program and labels every metric as that customer's result.

## Narrative and information architecture

### 1. Hero: make movement easy to join and meaningful to measure

The headline makes the dual promise: broad employee access plus evidence HR can use. The hero visual deliberately combines an employee challenge card and an HR participation pulse. This avoids positioning the product as either a consumer step counter or a back-office dashboard.

### 2. Trust strip: remove rollout friction early

Before asking the buyer to explore features, the page addresses four rollout anxieties: no required wearable, global time zones, leaderboard privacy, and bounded admin visibility. Trust is part of adoption, not a late compliance appendix.

### 3. Format studio: choose the behavior, not just the theme

The signature interaction is a four-format chooser. Each option states the behavior it is best at creating, the employee experience, the HR setting, and a suggested use pattern from product documentation. This turns feature depth into a buying decision.

### 4. Participation loop: make the next step visible

Three moments explain why people continue: passive step sync reduces logging work; progress, teams, and milestones give effort a social shape; optional recognition reinforces completion. The section also makes the non-competitive path explicit.

### 5. HR command center: launch, nudge, prove

An enterprise program manager needs to see the operating model. The mock dashboard pairs a participation trend, department view, participant management, and export action with four concise workflow steps.

### 6. Customer proof: Brazosport ISD “Fit Wars”

The case study is closely matched to the use case and has recent, named outcomes: 86% employee engagement, 132 active participants, and 6,000+ average daily steps. The quote is attributed to the Director of Benefits & Wellness. The page avoids presenting this as a guaranteed result.

### 7. Objection handling and final CTA

Five FAQs answer the questions most likely to stop evaluation: wearables, teams, privacy, global operation, and reporting. The closing CTA describes what the buyer will see in a demo.

### Format studio

**Eyebrow:** Four step-program formats

**H2:** Choose the behavior you want to create.

**Body:** Start with a proven format, then set the audience, dates, and experience around your workforce.

**Race**

- Label: Spark a short burst
- Headline: Make the goal instantly clear: walk the most.
- Body: A pure steps leaderboard with no daily target or extra tasks. Best for a fast, visible company sprint.
- Employee view: Total steps and live rank
- HR sets: Audience and dates
- Suggested pattern: 1–2 weeks

**Streak**

- Label: Build daily consistency
- Headline: Reward the days people show up.
- Body: Employees aim for one daily step target. Rankings prioritize completed days, with total steps breaking ties.
- Employee view: Daily target, completed days, and streak
- HR sets: Target, teams, audience, and dates
- Suggested pattern: 2–4 weeks

**Journey**

- Label: Make progress visual
- Headline: Turn cumulative steps into a route worth finishing.
- Body: Employees move through milestones on a themed map, earning points as they advance—individually or with a team.
- Employee view: Current position and next milestone
- HR sets: Template, milestone targets, teams, audience, and dates
- Suggested pattern: Multi-week program

**E-Marathon**

- Label: Rally around a shared event
- Headline: Convert everyday steps into meaningful distance.
- Body: Steps become kilometers or miles on the leaderboard, so a distributed workforce can take part in one virtual event.
- Employee view: Distance covered and rank
- HR sets: Conversion ratio, distance unit, audience, and dates
- Suggested pattern: Fitness week, wellness month, or company event

### Participation loop

**Eyebrow:** Designed for follow-through

**H2:** Make the first step easy—and the next one visible.

**Body:** Participation grows when logging disappears into the background, progress feels personal, and shared momentum stays close at hand.

1. **Sync without a new device.** Steps can flow from a phone, Apple Health, Fitbit, Garmin, and supported Android device paths. One primary source helps prevent double-counting.
2. **Give effort a visible destination.** Show the next milestone, the current week, overall progress, or a team’s standing—not just a raw number at day’s end.
3. **Create fair social momentum.** Run individual or team experiences where supported. Team standings use average member scores, so contribution matters more than team size.
4. **Recognize progress your way.** Add milestone points, completion recognition, notifications, or rewards when enabled—or run a simple challenge without them.

**Privacy note:** Prefer progress without the pressure? Employees can hide from individual rankings while their activity still counts toward the challenge and team.

### HR command center

**Eyebrow:** Control for HR and wellbeing teams

**H2:** Launch in minutes. Keep momentum. Leave with proof.

**Body:** Run the program from one admin workflow instead of stitching together device data, spreadsheets, and winner updates.

1. **Set the experience.** Choose a format, dates, public or private visibility, and a company-wide or filtered audience. A standard challenge can be configured in 5–10 minutes.
2. **Bring people in.** Add individuals, bulk upload participants, invite employees, and configure teams where the format supports them.
3. **Read the room while it is live.** Monitor step, score, and team standings; search or filter; and send participant notifications when momentum needs a lift.
4. **Close with a credible result.** Allow for final device syncs, then export leaderboard results—including participant, department, score, steps, and rank—to CSV.

### FAQs

**Do employees need a smartwatch or fitness tracker?**  
No. A phone can provide step data through Apple Health on iPhone or the supported Android step path. Employees can also connect supported wearables such as Fitbit, Garmin, or Apple Watch.

**Can we run a team challenge without favoring the largest team?**  
Yes, in formats that support teams. Team standings are based on average member scores rather than total team size. Race is intentionally an individual, pure-steps format; choose Streak, Journey, or a Custom challenge when team configuration is needed.

**What can HR see about employees?**  
Admins can see participation status, challenge rankings, team scores, registration status, last-active date, and aggregate program measures. They cannot see individual weight, health assessment answers, lab results, food diaries, mood logs, or sleep records.

**Can one challenge work across countries and time zones?**  
Yes. Challenge start, end, and daily reset are localized to each participant’s time zone, so every employee receives the same number of calendar days. Admin report date filters use the admin’s time zone.

**What can we report after the challenge?**  
HR can export the filtered leaderboard to CSV with participant, department, score, step, team, and ranking fields. The dashboard also provides live step, score, and team leaderboard views.

## Visual direction

- **System:** Reuse `../styles/enterprise.css`, Noto Sans, and the homepage’s ink/coral/mint palette.
- **Mood:** Precise enterprise product design with energetic movement cues; not fitness-influencer imagery.
- **Hero signature:** Interlocking employee phone and HR pulse panels connected by one coral movement path. The relationship—employee action becoming an HR-readable signal—is the visual thesis.
- **Format signature:** A true tab interface rather than four equal feature cards. The active format changes the goal, description, employee view, admin controls, and recommended pattern.
- **Section rhythm:** Light editorial hero → white trust strip → dark product-format studio → light participation narrative → ink admin command center → warm customer proof → compact FAQ and CTA.
- **Data integrity:** All product UI figures are marked as illustrative. Customer metrics are visually separated and named.
- **Accessibility:** Semantic landmarks and headings, visible focus, keyboard-operable tabs and FAQ buttons, sufficient contrast, reduced-motion handling, and mobile navigation.
