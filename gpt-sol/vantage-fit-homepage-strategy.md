# Vantage Fit homepage redesign

## Executive recommendation

Reposition Vantage Fit around one enterprise promise:

> **Turn employee wellbeing into measurable participation.**

The homepage should help a senior HR buyer answer five questions in five screens:

1. Is this built to improve participation?
2. Why will employees keep using it?
3. Can HR measure and improve the program?
4. Has it worked for organizations like mine?
5. Will it pass enterprise security and privacy review?

The live site already contains many of the right ingredients, but it distributes them across a long, feature-heavy story. The shortlisted prototype improves the participation narrative, but expands it into roughly eleven body sections and omits two late-stage buying requirements: customer video proof and enterprise trust.

The recommended homepage uses five `100svh`-style sections on large screens, natural-height sections on mobile, one primary CTA, and progressive disclosure through tabs, video, navigation, and an AI buyer assistant. Navigation and supporting pages carry the detailed inventory; the homepage carries the decision story.

## Working assumptions and claim discipline

- Primary audience: US enterprise CHROs, Benefits leaders, Total Rewards leaders, Wellbeing leaders, and their HR/IT/security evaluation partners.
- Primary conversion: qualified enterprise demo request.
- North-star product metric: participation rate.
- Recommended homepage definition: **unique enrolled employees completing a qualifying activity or challenge action during the selected period, divided by enrolled employees**.
- The `68%` dashboard shown in the concept is illustrative product data, not a portfolio-wide claim.
- Customer outcomes must always remain named and time-bounded. Do not turn the Tata Motors, IBS Software, or Brazosport ISD results into an “average customer result.”
- The current site and help content refer to HIPAA, GDPR, SOC 2, ISO 27001, and ISO 27701. Final certification wording, certificate status, and badge usage should be approved by Security/Legal before launch.
- “100+ organizations” is used on the live homepage, while another internal audit found inconsistent organization counts elsewhere. Either validate one company-wide number or use a safer line such as “Trusted by HR teams at…” with named logos.

# 1. Homepage audit and recommendations

## A. Messaging weaknesses

### What works

- The current hero is materially better than a generic “healthier, happier workforce” message. “The wellness platform employees actually use” and “launched in days, not months” address adoption and implementation friction.
- Participation is present in the hero subcopy.
- Named customer outcomes are stronger than category-level wellness statistics: Tata Motors at 59% engagement and IBS Software at 88% engagement.
- “Challenges, rewards, and experiences employees want to be part of” is a useful product mechanism.
- The shortlisted prototype makes participation the visible product north star and connects the employee app with the HR dashboard.

### What needs to improve

1. **The hero does not make the enterprise buyer explicit.**  
   The current copy could address a 200-person company or a global enterprise. Add “enterprise HR teams” and make global rollout, measurement, and privacy visible early.

2. **The value proposition stops at use, rather than business control.**  
   “Employees actually use” is an excellent opening, but senior buyers also need: “HR can prove adoption, find participation gaps, and improve the program.”

3. **The first major H2 is generic.**  
   “How Corporate Wellness programs help?” is awkward, category-level copy. It does not advance the Vantage Fit story.

4. **“Cue > Action > Reward” is internal product logic, not buyer value.**  
   The model is useful, but it should be translated into a participation loop: relevant invitation, visible progress, meaningful reward, measurable improvement.

5. **Feature copy describes the platform more than the buyer outcome.**  
   The five large feature tabs explain what exists, but not how each capability increases participation, reduces HR administration, or improves executive reporting.

6. **Analytics language is broad.**  
   “Actionable insights” and “assess program success” are less persuasive than the actual dashboard story: participation snapshot → trend versus target → department breakdown → recommended action.

7. **Some claims create proof risk.**  
   “One defensible ROI number for your CFO” is stronger than the evidence currently presented. The `$250 healthcare savings` item has no destination in the current homepage content. Remove it from the primary proof layer unless the methodology and source are published.

8. **The Vantage Circle platform advantage is missing.**  
   If strategically important, supporting copy or the footer can note that Vantage Fit is part of the Vantage Circle employee experience suite. It should not distract from the single-product homepage promise.

## B. UX/UI issues

1. **The navigation is overpopulated.**  
   The live mega menus expose roughly 10 Solutions, 14 Features, and many Resources before the visitor has chosen a path. The categories mix use cases, challenge formats, product features, and technical capabilities.

2. **The page has too many equally weighted sections.**  
   Hero, statistics, habit loop, feature tabs, testimonials, “Why Vantage Fit,” a second video, security, blogs, and final CTA all compete for attention. The shortlisted prototype expands this further with separate problem, engine, experience, measurement, proof, responsible participation, buying, FAQ, and final CTA sections.

3. **The visual hierarchy does not track the buying journey.**  
   Security is a badge strip rather than a buyer-grade explanation. Customer videos appear after substantial scrolling. Blog content receives almost the same page weight as customer evidence.

4. **Product value is fragmented across decorative visuals.**  
   Tall “Cue / Action / Reward” cards and the large feature-tab scene consume substantial vertical space without showing the employee-to-HR feedback loop.

5. **Motion and dark/glow treatments can overpower the content.**  
   Count-up animations, card entrances, hero tilt, glows, tab backgrounds, and carousels create energy, but also add cognitive and performance cost. Enterprise buyers benefit from calm, precise motion attached to product meaning.

6. **Mobile relies on horizontal carousels and accordions for core content.**  
   This hides comparison and makes the story harder to scan. The new structure should stack the five decisions naturally on mobile.

7. **Security details are too far from the badge claim.**  
   A US enterprise evaluator needs to understand regional hosting, personal-health-data boundaries, encryption, SSO, and implementation—not only see logos.

## C. Conversion opportunities

1. Keep **Book a demo** as the single primary CTA throughout.
2. Add a proof-oriented secondary CTA in the hero: **Watch a customer story**.
3. Explain the demo before the final CTA: employee experience, sample challenge, participation dashboard, security requirements.
4. Move a US Benefits leader’s video story above the security section.
5. Pair qualitative video proof with one verified, time-bounded outcome.
6. Add **Review security & privacy** as a late-stage buyer path.
7. Replace the full homepage blog block with one Resources link in navigation and footer.
8. Offer an AI buyer assistant for high-intent questions such as participation measurement, HR visibility into health data, SSO, integrations, rollout, and pricing.
9. Track the buyer journey, not just demo clicks:
   - hero demo click
   - customer video play and completion
   - security-detail click
   - pricing click
   - employee/admin product toggle
   - AI assistant question category
   - demo form start and completion

## D. Content to retain

- Participation-led hero direction.
- “Launched in days, not months,” subject to implementation validation.
- Named customer outcomes:
  - Tata Motors: 59% engagement in a six-month pilot.
  - IBS Software: 88% engagement in a month-long challenge.
  - Brazosport ISD: 86% engagement in the two-week Fit Wars initiative.
- Rachel Arthur, Shyam Surendran, and Matt Whitmore customer videos and approved quotes.
- Inclusive challenges, team formats, rewards, and participation analytics.
- The dual employee and HR experience.
- Customer logo proof.
- Security/compliance badges and the “Your data is safe with us” intent.
- Help-center-supported capabilities: 65+ activity types, multiple challenge formats, cohort targeting, branded communications, CSV reporting, SSO, HRIS/SFTP setup, mobile/web access, and supported wearable connections.
- The dashboard’s north-star spine: snapshot → target → trend → breakdown → action.

# 2. Information architecture for the new homepage

## Story architecture

| Screen | Buyer question | Message | Primary evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Participation hero | “Is this relevant to my priority?” | Vantage Fit turns employee wellbeing into measurable participation. | Participation dashboard + enterprise logos |
| 2. Participation engine | “Why will employees keep using it?” | Inclusive programs, visible progress, team momentum, and rewards create repeat participation. | Employee experience + HR program controls |
| 3. Measurement and action | “Can HR prove and improve it?” | Start with participation, diagnose gaps, and take the next action. | Target, trend, department breakdown, Leadership Insight |
| 4. Customer video proof | “Has this worked for real HR teams?” | Benefits and business leaders describe sustained use and outcomes. | Three approved videos + named, time-bounded results |
| 5. Enterprise trust and conversion | “Will it pass review and scale?” | Regional hosting, privacy boundaries, security controls, SSO/integrations, and guided rollout. | Compliance badges + privacy explanation + tailored demo |

## Page-level messaging hierarchy

1. **Category:** Enterprise employee wellness platform.
2. **Differentiated promise:** Employees choose to participate; HR can measure and improve that participation.
3. **Mechanism:** Inclusive challenges + communications + team momentum + meaningful rewards.
4. **Operational value:** Launch, target, manage, and report from one admin experience.
5. **Proof:** Named customer outcomes and Benefits-leader videos.
6. **Trust:** Regional data residency, aggregate reporting, personal health privacy, security program, SSO/integrations.

# 6. UX/UI rationale

## Why screen 1 exists

Senior buyers decide quickly whether a page understands their priority. The hero makes the business problem and the measurable outcome the same idea. A real product-shaped dashboard gives the participation claim substance, while the employee-app overlay shows that the metric is created by an experience employees actually use.

The secondary CTA goes to customer evidence rather than generic product detail. This serves buyers who need trust before they will book.

## Why screen 2 exists

Participation cannot be presented as a dashboard metric alone. This screen explains the mechanism that creates it. The three-step loop is more useful than a feature inventory because it connects product capability to behavior.

The employee/HR toggle demonstrates the platform’s two-sided value without creating two separate sections. Employees get relevance and momentum; HR gets control and manageability.

## Why screen 3 exists

Enterprise HR leaders need a defensible measurement model and a way to act on it. The screen mirrors the admin dashboard’s strongest information architecture:

```text
Participation now → trend vs target → cohort gap → recommended action
```

The page deliberately keeps the Organizational Wellness Score as a supporting measure, not the hero claim. Participation is easier to explain to a CHRO, CFO, or Benefits committee.

The privacy line appears next to analytics because that is exactly when a buyer asks, “What can HR see?”

## Why screen 4 exists

Video reduces the perceived risk of buying from a less familiar provider. Rachel Arthur is the strongest lead for the US audience because her Benefits & Wellness role and school-district context closely match the target buyer. The outcome chip converts a qualitative endorsement into a business proof point without rewriting the customer’s words.

Two secondary videos provide breadth without requiring a separate carousel section. Tata Motors and IBS Software add quantified global-enterprise evidence.

## Why screen 5 exists

Security cannot remain a logo strip for a US enterprise sale. This screen translates badges into buyer-relevant assurances: residency, privacy boundaries, access, integrations, and rollout. Combining trust and the final CTA means the last objection and the next action sit together.

## Visual direction

- Preserve the shortlisted prototype’s dark charcoal, coral, mint, and lime palette, but use lime as a signal color for participation—not as decoration everywhere.
- Use a calm editorial grid and fewer floating elements.
- Let product data and customer faces carry the visual interest.
- Use one dashboard visualization language across hero and analytics.
- Use real customer thumbnails and company logos in the proof section.
- Prefer subtle transitions tied to state changes: tab switch, chart reveal, video play, AI assistant open.
- Avoid auto-advancing carousels and long count-up animations.
- Use `min-height`, not fixed height, for “full-screen” sections.
- Respect reduced-motion settings and full keyboard operation.
- Keep line length around 55–70 characters for body copy.
- Maintain WCAG 2.2 AA contrast, visible focus states, captions/transcripts for every video, and semantic heading order.

## Responsive behavior

- Desktop: five visual screens with a subtle progress indicator.
- Tablet: two-column layouts become balanced stacked groups; do not shrink product UI below legibility.
- Mobile: natural-height sections; horizontal step loop becomes three stacked cards; employee/HR toggle remains; customer videos stack; trust cards become a single column.
- Never trap scroll with mandatory scroll snapping. Optional proximity snapping may be used only on large screens.

## Conversion design

- One filled CTA treatment: Book a demo.
- One secondary outline treatment: proof or security.
- Text links for Pricing and deeper feature exploration.
- Repeat the primary CTA in screens 1, 3, and 5 only.
- Video CTA is a play action, not styled as another sales CTA.
- Demo request page should carry the same promise and state what the buyer will see.

## AI-first experience rationale

An AI-first marketing site should reduce evaluation effort, not merely add animated generative decoration. The assistant is valuable because Vantage Fit already has detailed help, security, pricing, and customer content. Retrieval-grounded answers turn that content into an on-demand enterprise sales engineer while preserving the five-screen homepage.

The site can also remember an explicitly selected lens—CHRO, Benefits, Wellbeing, or IT/Security—and reorder suggested prompts or proof. It should not silently infer sensitive traits or rewrite core claims per visitor.
