Why programs stall

Wellbeing programs rarely fail on content.

They fail on three things that have nothing to do with the library.

The content waits to be opened.

A library only produces usage when someone decides to open it.

Joining is a disclosure.

Anything labeled mental health asks the people who need it most to be seen.

The only number is utilization.

Leadership asks what moved, and the answer is a license count.

What this is

A habit and participation layer, run as a scored challenge, at whole-workforce scale.

What this is not

An EAP, counseling, a therapist network, a helpline, or a clinical screening tool. This sits under them in your benefits stack.

The fix for all three is the same: put wellbeing in the task list everyone is already enrolled in.

For employees

Nobody signs up, and nobody quietly drops out.

Three mechanics get people in without a sign-up. Three more keep a modest logger within reach of the top until the last day.

Enrolled, not invited

An audience rule enrolls everyone who matches, or you add people by search or CSV upload. There is no browse-and-join flow.

Nothing to disclose to take part

Health-profile onboarding is skippable past the health connection, so weight and BMI are optional.

No wearable needed

Steps come from Apple Health on iOS and Google Fit on Android, with no wearable required.

The keenest meditator cannot run away with the leaderboard

Wellbeing tasks earn points up to 100% of the target and no further, so five yoga sessions against a target of three still score three.

A bad week is not permanent

The weekly leaderboard resets each Monday, while the overall view stays cumulative.

A reminder the employee sets, not one HR pushes

Mood is one of six reminder types. The employee picks the time and the repeat days.

Missed daily check-ins cannot be back-filled, so set targets people can hit.

Custom habits stay in the employee's Plus menu whether or not a challenge is running.

Privacy and stigma

Participation visible. Feelings invisible.

The reason people take part in this one is that taking part reveals nothing.

Mood is private to the employee

From the published help doc: “Your mood data is private to you. It is not visible to your HR admin, manager, or colleagues.”

Hide from the leaderboard, stay in the program

Opt-out hides an employee from rankings, but they still participate, still earn points, and still count toward their team's average.

Any filter makes the challenge private

Applying an audience filter makes the challenge visible only to employees who match it.

Cohorts come back as a count, not a list

The audience screen returns a number and the line “The list of users is not displayed to protect individual privacy.”

Privacy, reporting and rollout

Questions before rollout.

Can HR see an employee's mood entries?

No. Mood data is private to the employee and never appears on a leaderboard. A mood check-in can be scored, so the score shows only that it happened.

What does HR get to report on?

Participation, completion and a per-task breakdown for each employee. Not a mood report, not a mood trend, and not a health breakdown by department: workforce health reporting is organization-level only.

Do employees have to sign up?

No. An audience rule enrolls everyone who matches, or you add people by search or CSV upload. Someone added after the start scores from that date forward, so a late add gives nobody an advantage.

Does this replace our EAP?

No. Vantage Fit does not provide counseling, a therapist network, a helpline, or clinical screening. Run it alongside the EAP contract you already hold, as the daily habit layer underneath it.

Will this work for employees without a wearable or a desk?

Yes. Mood, sleep and water log from the web as well as the app, and guided sessions play offline and sync later. Steps need no wearable.
