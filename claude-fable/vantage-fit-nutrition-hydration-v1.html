Features · For employees

An employee nutrition tracking app that shows its working.

The daily target is not a round number somebody picked. It comes from Mifflin-St Jeor, an activity multiplier and the goal the employee chose, then splits across four meals. Water logs one fixed 250 ml glass at a time.

The math

Mifflin-St Jeor, then the goal.

Four steps, and a floor that stops the number going somewhere unsafe.

1
Resting burn

Mifflin-St Jeor, from height, weight, date of birth and gender.

2
Activity multiplier

Sedentary 1.2, light 1.375, moderate 1.55, heavy 1.725, very heavy 1.9.

3
Goal adjustment

Plus or minus 550 calories a day, which is roughly half a kilo a week.

4
Safety floor

The target never drops below 1,800 for men or 1,200 for women.

Macros by goalProtein / carbs / fat

All of it comes from the basic onboarding profile. The Health Risk Assessment is not required for a calorie or macro target.
Food logging

Breakfast 25. Lunch 35. Snacks 10. Dinner 30.

Corporate wellness food logging usually stops at a daily total. A day is lost at lunch, not at midnight, so the target is split before the employee opens the app.

25%Breakfast
35%Lunch
10%Snacks
30%Dinner
Pick the meal, then search the food or tap it from the Quick Tray of things logged often. Set the serving and the quantity. Calories with protein, carbs and fat update as they change. The entry lands against that meal's target, not just the daily one.
Hydration

One tap is one glass. 250 ml.

The glass size is fixed, so two employees who logged eight glasses logged the same amount. That is what makes employee hydration tracking comparable across a workforce.

The default target is 8 glasses, which is 2 litres. An exact amount can be typed instead, in litres or fluid ounces. A challenge task can set its own target, for example 3.0 litres on four days this week.
250 250 250 250 250 250 250 250

8 glasses = 2 litres. The default target when no challenge is running.

What it is not

A few thousand foods, and a way around it.

The database is roughly 3,000 to 4,000 items

It is being expanded. Regional dishes and home cooking are the known gaps, and we would rather name them.

Manual entry needs no database

A name, a calorie count, and macros if they are known. An estimate that gets logged beats a meal that does not.

Suggest a food

Employees submit what is missing, and the team reviews it for a later database update.

None of this is AI

Search is search, and the target is a formula you can check by hand.

Mobile only, and Full Mode only

Food and water logging live in the mobile app, not the web app. Neither exists in Lite Mode, which is steps and Race challenges.

A logged meal counts the way a run does.

Water and nutrition tasks sit in the same challenge builder as steps and workouts, and land in the same participation rate your reports read.

Rollout questions

Three things buyers ask in week one.

Does someone have to finish the Health Risk Assessment first?

No. Height, weight, date of birth, gender, goal and activity level from onboarding are enough for a daily target and a macro split.

Can an employee log lunch from a laptop?

No. Food and water logging are in the mobile app. The web app covers challenges and leaderboards.

Does HR see what someone ate?

No. Admins see aggregates. An individual food diary is never open to an admin.
