Why baseline matters

Challenges without a health baseline are half a story.

Step counts and challenge join rates show effort. They do not show whether the workforce is starting from a healthy foundation.

01

Neutral default

Without HRA, baseline defaults to 50. Your Org Wellness Score is incomplete until employees complete a real assessment.

02

Blind targeting

Programs stay one-size-fits-all when risk factors are unknown. High-BMI and sedentary groups need different playbooks.

03

Trust barrier

Employees will not share health data if HR can open individual files. Privacy has to be product architecture, not a footer promise.

Questions before rollout

Straight answers on privacy, score, and control.

Is the HRA a medical diagnosis?
No. It is an informational wellness tool that produces a fitness percentage and category from self-reported data. For any health concerns, employees should consult their clinician.
Can HR see individual employee answers?
No. Individual HRA responses are private to the employee. HR works with aggregate risk distribution and program-level views, not personal questionnaire fields.
What if employees skip the HRA?
Completion is voluntary. Without HRA, the Wellness Score uses a default baseline of 50. The rest of the platform still works. Completing HRA simply makes the baseline component accurate.
How does HRA affect the Org Wellness Score?
HRA feeds the Baseline component, weighted at 20% of the Org Wellness Score. The other components are Participation (30%), Activity (30%), and Adherence (20%). More accurate baselines make the overall score more trustworthy for leadership reporting.
Can employees retake the assessment?
Yes. When health situation changes, employees can retake the HRA so their fitness percentage and baseline update. That keeps personal Wellness Score and org-level baseline inputs current.
